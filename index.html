About

My research centers on differential geometry and its interactions with topology, rigidity, and dynamics. I am particularly interested in how curvature and global geometric structure constrain dynamical behavior.

I also work at the interface of mathematics, computation, and artificial intelligence, connecting mathematical theory to challenging applied problems. I earned my Ph.D. from the University of Michigan and previously chaired the Department of Mathematics at Indiana University.

PERSONAL

I am married to Shelly Connell and am the father of seven children, six living. We live in Bloomington, Indiana, and worship at Trinity Reformed Church.

Applied mathematics & AI

My applied work has included dynamical network prediction, spectral analysis, integral transforms, and remote sensing. I am interested in moving between mathematical principles and applications in which quantitative modeling can make a concrete difference.

These interests connect with my work in AI and analytics for complex real-world data, including risk and prediction problems.

Research

My work is motivated by structural questions in geometry, dynamics, and topology, often connecting global theorems with geometric and analytic methods.

Geometry & rigidity

Curvature, locally symmetric spaces, geodesic flows, and rigidity phenomena, including higher-rank and rank-one settings.

Geometry & topology

Simplicial volume, geometric invariants, and the relationships between geometric structures and topology.

Dynamics & ergodic theory

Geometric dynamics, random walks, and the interplay among invariant structures, flows, and global geometry.

Applied mathematics & AI

Predictive modeling, dynamical network prediction, spectral analysis, integral transforms, and remote sensing.
